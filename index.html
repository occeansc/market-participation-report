How it began. A first retail account traded through the 2020 COVID crash. Sustained trading operations resumed in September 2023 and have run continuously since — 36 months, or three full years, through September 2026. Counting 2020, the record spans five calendar years in total.

How it broadened. From 2023, trading operations widened beyond currencies (foreign exchange, or FX) to oil and gold, then in 2024 to silver, US index futures, Bitcoin and Nigerian equities, and in 2025 to US equities. Three paths have been used: retail accounts; accounts with proprietary trading firms (“prop firms”), each beginning as a paid evaluation of trading ability; and the US Equity Account, a longer-horizon US stock account. Together that is 21 accounts and 2,124 trades.

Prop firms. 17 prop-firm accounts were run, paying $988 in fees. They provided $391,000 of account capacity and produced an account-level result of −4.1%. Two stages were passed; no payouts have been received.

The equity accounts. Both are active. The US Equity Account, trading on the NYSE and Nasdaq, earned +$681 on $1,000 (68%). The Nigerian Equity Account, traded on the Nigerian Exchange (NGX), earned +₦92,037 net of fees on ₦220,793 of capital at its peak (42%).

Current position. By September 2026 the net result stood at −$858. 2025 and 2026 each finished the year net positive, led by the two equity accounts. An interim low of −$1,674 occurred in Mar 2026, when new prop-firm fees were paid before that year’s gains came through. Capital came from personal (25%), friend (48%) and family (26%) sources, of which 60% went into US instruments, 35% into global Spot FX and 5% into Nigerian equities. ₦400,000 of ₦720,000 has been repaid to one family lender, and the US Equity Account has paid out $1,498 since it opened.

These figures are on a cash basis: prop-firm fees paid, plus results from closed trades in the retail and equity accounts. Trading result inside the evaluation accounts themselves is measured against account size on the performance pages.

8 · Research approach

The approach is pattern recognition, and trading has become more selective: 131 trades per account in 2024, 27 in 2026

Primary approach. Generating value from the markets through the application of the art, science and technology of pattern recognition.

Art

Recognising patterns on the chart as they form, and reading what they suggest in the moment.

Science

Testing whether a pattern repeats with an edge: samples, expectancy and risk limits rather than anecdote.

Technology

Encoding what works into indicators, dashboards and automated systems so it can be applied without drift.

Supporting lenses

Notes

Basis of preparation

Basis of the figures. Retail accounts show the account’s realised result; prop firms show the evaluation fee paid, not the evaluation account’s own profit or loss; funds show trading result before payouts to those with a stake in the account. Evaluation-account profit and loss is reported separately, as a memo item.

Currency. Reported in USD. Nigerian Equity Account results are recorded in naira and converted to USD at approximate monthly ₦/$ levels: recorded rates for 2024 and published Central Bank of Nigeria levels for 2025–26 (about ₦1,360–1,530). Family amounts are shown in naira as recorded.

Nigerian Equity Account. Gains are shown net of fees: ₦111,942 gross, less ₦19,905 in fees, for ₦92,037 net. One open position (6 shares of Geregu Power, bought for ₦5,400) has not been sold and is excluded from the results; it was running ₦446 below cost. This account has no separate deposit log; its capital base is the deepest point the running trade balance reaches — ₦220,793, reached in March 2026. ₦150,000 of that is family capital (BA); the remaining ₦70,793 is personal.

Data gaps. A few accounts rely on account-level summaries rather than complete trade-by-trade records — Alpari 2020, two of the MyFundedFX evaluations, and Alpari 2023. These are minor gaps that don't affect the totals shown.

Other notes. No prop payouts, no other accounts, and no participation in 2021–22.

Sources. Account and trade-level portfolio records, Nigerian Equity Account trade records, and the family credit and repayment log. Event dates are from public reporting. Data as of September 2026.

Terms. A prop firm is a proprietary trading firm that lets a trader use its account after passing a paid evaluation. Spot FX is trading one currency against another for settlement at the current rate; futures are standardised contracts on an index or commodity. A CFD is a contract for difference, which tracks a price without owning the asset. NGX is the Nigerian Exchange, where Nigerian equities are traded. Alpari is the online broker used for the two retail accounts.
