How it began. The journey started in March 2020, when a first personal account was opened as COVID volatility peaked. After a pause, participation resumed in September 2023 and has been continuous since, so it now spans five calendar years: 2020, and 2023 to 2026.

How it broadened. From 2023 the journey widened beyond currencies (foreign exchange, or FX) to oil and gold, then in 2024 to silver, US index futures, Bitcoin and Nigerian stocks, and in 2025 to US stocks. Three paths in have been used: personal accounts; accounts with proprietary trading firms (“prop firms”), each beginning as a paid evaluation of trading ability; and the US Equity Account, a longer-horizon US stock account. Together that is 19 accounts and 2,124 trades.

Prop firms. 15 prop-firm accounts were run, paying $1,016 in fees. They provided $291,000 of account capacity and produced an account-level result of −5.4%. Two stages were passed; no payouts have been received.

The equity accounts. Both are active. The US Equity Account, trading on the NYSE and Nasdaq, earned +$681 on $1,000 (68%). The Nigerian stock account, traded on the Nigerian Exchange (NGX), earned ₦92,037 net of fees on ₦220,793 of capital at its peak (42%).

Current position. By September 2026 the net result stood at −$886. 2025 and 2026 each finished the year net positive, led by the two equity accounts. An interim low of −$1,702 occurred in Mar 2026, when new prop-firm fees were paid before that year’s gains came through. The journey drew on personal (24%), friend (49%) and family (27%) capital, of which 61% went into US instruments, 35% into global FX and 4% into Nigerian stocks. ₦400,000 of ₦720,000 has been repaid to one family lender, and the US Equity Account has paid out $1,458 since it opened.

Figures reflect money that actually moved: prop-firm fees paid, and results from closed trades in personal accounts and the two equity accounts. Evaluation-account profit and loss, which stayed inside the evaluation and was never received, is reported separately as a memo item.

8 · Research approach

The approach is pattern recognition, and trading has become more selective: 164 trades per account in 2024, 27 in 2026

Primary approach. Generating value from the markets through the application of the art, science and technology of pattern recognition.

Art

Reading structure, context and behaviour in price: the discretionary judgement of what a pattern means now.

Science

Testing whether a pattern repeats with an edge: samples, expectancy and risk limits rather than anecdote.

Technology

Encoding what works into indicators, dashboards and automated systems so it can be applied without drift.

Supporting lenses

LensRole
Technical analysisStructure, levels, momentum and reversal signals across FX, futures and stocks
FundamentalsSector and company selection for the equity accounts, including thematic sector intelligence
EventsMacro and geopolitical catalysts: rates, elections, wars, supply shocks (see events, page 4)

Notes

Basis of preparation

Basis of the figures. Personal accounts show the account’s realised result; prop firms show the evaluation fee paid, not the evaluation account’s own profit or loss; funds show trading result before payouts to those with a stake in the account. Evaluation-account profit and loss is reported separately, as a memo item.

Currency. Reported in USD. Nigerian stock results are recorded in naira and converted to USD at approximate monthly ₦/$ levels: recorded rates for 2024 and published Central Bank of Nigeria levels for 2025–26 (about ₦1,360–1,530). Family amounts are shown in naira as recorded.

Nigerian stocks. Gains are shown net of fees: ₦111,942 gross, less ₦19,905 in fees, for ₦92,037 net. One open position (6 shares of Geregu Power, bought for ₦5,400) has not been sold and is excluded from the results; it was running ₦446 below cost. This account has no separate deposit log, so its capital is estimated from the trade record itself: the largest amount ever tied up at one time, tracking each purchase and sale in order. That peak, ₦220,793, was reached in March 2026 and is used as the capital base for its return.

Data gaps. A few accounts rely on account-level summaries rather than complete trade-by-trade records — Alpari 2020, two of the MyFundedFX evaluations, and Alpari 2023. These differences are minor and do not change the overall picture.

Other notes. No prop payouts, no other accounts, and no participation in 2021–22.

Sources. Account and trade-level portfolio records, Nigerian stock trade records, and the family credit and repayment log. Event dates are from public reporting. Data as of September 2026.

Terms. A prop firm is a proprietary trading firm that lets a trader use its account after passing a paid evaluation. FX is trading one currency against another; futures are standardised contracts on an index or commodity. A CFD is a contract for difference, which tracks a price without owning the asset. NGX is the Nigerian Exchange, where Nigerian stocks are traded. Alpari is the online broker used for the two personal accounts.
