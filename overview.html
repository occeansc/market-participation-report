How it began. Activity in these markets began with a personal account in 2020. The current, continuous phase started in September 2023 and has run for 36 months, or three full years, through September 2026.

How it broadened. From 2023, activity widened beyond currencies (foreign exchange, or FX) to oil and gold, then in 2024 to silver, US index futures, Bitcoin and Nigerian equities, and in 2025 to US equities. Three paths have been used: personal accounts; accounts with proprietary trading firms (“prop firms”), each beginning as a paid evaluation of trading ability; and the US Equity Account, a longer-horizon US stock account. Together that is 18 accounts and 2,124 trades.

Prop firms. 15 prop-firm accounts were run, paying $1,016 in fees. They provided $291,000 of account capacity and produced an account-level result of −5.4%. Two stages were passed; no payouts have been received.

The equity accounts. Both are active. The US Equity Account, trading on the NYSE and Nasdaq, earned +$681 on $1,000 (68%). The Nigerian Equity Account, traded on the Nigerian Exchange (NGX), earned +₦92,037 net of fees on ₦220,793 of capital at its peak (42%).

Current position. By September 2026 the net result across the current phase stood at −$594. 2025 and 2026 each finished the year net positive, led by the two equity accounts.

Figures reflect money that actually moved: prop-firm fees paid, and results from closed trades in personal accounts and the two equity accounts. Evaluation-account profit and loss, which stayed inside the evaluation and was never received, is reported separately as a memo item.

6 · Research approach

The approach is pattern recognition, and trading has become more selective: 164 trades per account in 2024, 27 in 2026

Primary approach. Generating value from the markets through the application of the art, science and technology of pattern recognition.

Art

Recognising patterns on the chart as they form, and reading what they suggest in the moment.

Science

Testing whether a pattern repeats with an edge: samples, expectancy and risk limits rather than anecdote.

Technology

Encoding what works into indicators, dashboards and automated systems so it can be applied without drift.

Notes

Basis of preparation

Basis of the figures. Personal accounts show the account’s realised result; prop firms show the evaluation fee paid, not the evaluation account’s own profit or loss; equity accounts show trading result net of any payouts. Evaluation-account profit and loss is reported separately, as a memo item.

Currency. Reported in USD. Nigerian Equity Account results are recorded in naira and converted to USD at approximate monthly ₦/$ levels: recorded rates for 2024 and published Central Bank of Nigeria levels for 2025–26 (about ₦1,360–1,530).

Nigerian Equity Account. Gains are shown net of fees: ₦111,942 gross, less ₦19,905 in fees, for ₦92,037 net. One open position (6 shares of Geregu Power, bought for ₦5,400) has not been sold and is excluded from the results; it was running ₦446 below cost. This account has no separate deposit log, so its capital is estimated from the trade record itself: the largest amount ever tied up at one time, tracking each purchase and sale in order. That peak, ₦220,793, was reached in March 2026 and is used as the capital base for its return.

Scope. This overview covers the current, continuous phase of activity: September 2023 to date. An earlier personal account traded briefly in 2020; its figures are not included in the totals, tables or charts above.

Data gaps. A few accounts rely on account-level summaries rather than complete trade-by-trade records. These differences are minor and do not change the overall picture.

Other notes. No prop payouts and no other accounts beyond those shown here.

Sources. Account and trade-level portfolio records, and Nigerian Equity Account trade records. Event dates are from public reporting. Data as of September 2026.

Terms. A prop firm is a proprietary trading firm that lets a trader use its account after passing a paid evaluation. FX is trading one currency against another; futures are standardised contracts on an index or commodity. A CFD is a contract for difference, which tracks a price without owning the asset. NGX is the Nigerian Exchange, where Nigerian equities are traded.
