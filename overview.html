How it began. Activities in these markets originally began with a US domiciled retail brokerage account in 2020. The period in review started in September 2023 and has run for 36 months, through September 2026.

Scope of markets. From 2023, trading operations widened beyond currencies (foreign exchange, or FX) to oil and gold, then in 2024 to silver, US index futures, Bitcoin and Nigerian equities, and in 2025 to US equities. Three three paths have been used: retail accounts; accounts with proprietary trading firms (“prop firms”), each beginning as a paid evaluation of trading ability; and the Equity Accounts, a longer-horizon US and Nigeria stock account. Together that is 20 accounts and 2,124 trades.

Prop firms. Seventeen prop-firm accounts were run at a total cost of $988 in fees. They provided $391,000 of account capacity, these produced an account-level result of −4.1%. Two stages were passed; no payouts have been received.

The equity accounts. Both remain active. The US Equity Account, trading on the NYSE and Nasdaq, earned +$681 on $1,000 (68%). The Nigerian Equity Account, traded on the Nigerian Exchange (NGX), earned +₦92,037 net of fees on ₦220,793 of capital at its peak (42%).

Current position. By September 2026 the net result across all operations stood at –$566. Both 2025 and 2026 finished the year net positive, led by the two equity accounts.

These figures are on a cash basis: prop-firm fees paid, plus results from closed trades in the retail and equity accounts. Trading result inside the evaluation accounts themselves is measured against account size on the performance pages.

6 · Research approach

Trading has become more selective: 131 trades per account in 2024, 27 in 2026

Primary approach. Generating value from the markets through the application of the art, science and technology of pattern recognition.

Art

Recognising behaviours and patterns on the chart as they form, deciphering what they suggest.

Science

Testing whether a pattern repeats with an edge: samples, expectancy and risk limits rather than anecdote.

Technology

Encoding what works into indicators, dashboards and automated systems so it can be sustainably applied.

Notes

Basis of preparation

Basis of the figures. Retail accounts show the account’s realised result; prop firms show the evaluation fee paid, not the evaluation account’s own profit or loss; equity accounts show trading result net of any payouts. Evaluation-account profit and loss is reported separately, as a memo item.

Currency. Reported in USD. Nigerian Equity Account results are recorded in naira and converted to USD at approximate monthly ₦/$ levels: recorded rates for 2024 and published Central Bank of Nigeria levels for 2025–26 (about ₦1,360–1,530).

Nigerian Equity Account. Gains are shown net of fees: ₦111,942 gross, less ₦19,905 in fees, for ₦92,037 net. The deepest point the running trade balance reaches — ₦220,793, reached in March 2026.

Scope. This overview covers the period from September 2023 to date. An earlier retail account traded briefly in 2020, shown in the timeline on page 2; its figures are not included in the totals, tables or charts above.

Data gaps. A few accounts rely on account-level summaries rather than complete trade-by-trade records. These are minor gaps that don't affect the totals shown.

Sources. Account and trade-level portfolio records, and Nigerian Equity Account trade records. Event dates are from public reporting. Data as of September 2026.

Terms. A prop firm is a proprietary trading firm that lets a trader use its account after passing a paid evaluation. Spot FX is trading one currency against another for settlement at the current rate; futures are standardised contracts on an index or commodity. A CFD is a contract for difference, which tracks a price without owning the asset.
